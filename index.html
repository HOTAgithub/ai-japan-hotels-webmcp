Japan Travel Guide

Expert tips and destination guides for your Japan trip

Tokyo offers an incredible range of accommodation for international visitors, from ultra-modern high-rises to cozy business hotels. Here are our top picks for 2026, all bookable directly through our platform.

1. Shinjuku Hotel PARK INN - Located in the heart of Shinjuku, one of Tokyo's most vibrant districts. Perfect for nightlife, shopping, and transit access. Rating: 3.8/5. Around ¥8,500/night.

2. HOTEL MYSTAYS Asakusa - Steps from Senso-ji Temple and traditional atmosphere. Ideal for first-time visitors wanting to experience old Tokyo. Rating: 3.5/5. Around ¥12,600/night.

3. ELE hotel Ginza East - Situated near Tokyo's upscale Ginza district. Great for luxury shopping and fine dining. Rating: 3.6/5. Around ¥9,800/night.

4. Olympic Inn Azabu - In the sophisticated Azabu area, close to Roppongi. Excellent for business travelers and expats. Rating: 3.9/5. Around ¥10,200/night.

5. OLYMPIC INN Kanda - Budget-friendly option near Akihabara and Tokyo Station. Compact but well-equipped rooms. Rating: 3.5/5. Around ¥7,500/night.

Other notable mentions include Kiba no Tsuru Crane Hotel for its unique riverside location, FLEXSTAY INN Shirogane for extended stays, and Smile Hotel Tokyo Ayase Ekimae for value-conscious travelers. Most Tokyo hotels accept international credit cards and offer free Wi-Fi. Book early during cherry blossom season (March-April) as availability fills up fast.

Kyoto, Japan's cultural capital, offers distinct neighborhoods each with their own character. Choosing the right area to stay can make or break your trip. Here's our comprehensive neighborhood guide.

Gion & Higashiyama - The most atmospheric area, home to geisha houses, Kiyomizu-dera Temple, and traditional wooden machiya streets. Stay at Hotel Kuretakeso Kyoto Kiyomizu Gojo for easy access to eastern Kyoto's top sights. Walkable to Kodai-ji and Yasaka Shrine.

Karasuma & Shijo (Downtown) - The commercial heart of Kyoto with excellent bus and subway connections. HOTEL CUORE Kyoto Shijo Omiya and Smile Hotel Kyoto Karasumagojo offer convenient access to Nishiki Market and the shopping district. Best for food lovers and public transport users.

Kyoto Station Area - Ideal for day-trippers using the Shinkansen. KYOTO PLAZA HOTEL KYOTO STATION SOUTH provides easy access to bullet trains, while the station itself is an architectural landmark with dining and shopping. Perfect for first-time visitors.

Nijo Castle Area - Stay near Kyoto's most famous castle. Hotels like HOTEL LiVEMAX KYOTO NIJO-JO-KITA put you within walking distance of the castle gardens. Quieter than downtown but well-connected by bus.

For a design-focused stay, consider HOTEL ANTEROOM KYOTO or The Celecton Kyoto Horikawa Sanjo. Budget travelers can find clean business hotels like EN HOTEL Kyoto from around ¥7,000/night. Kyoto hotels tend to book out months in advance during autumn foliage season (November) and cherry blossom season, so plan ahead.

A trip to see Mt. Fuji is a bucket-list experience for many travelers. The surrounding Yamanashi and Shizuoka prefectures offer everything from luxury ryokan with private onsen to budget-friendly hotels with stunning mountain views.

Lake Kawaguchi Area - The most popular base for Mt. Fuji viewing. Hotels in the Kawaguchiko-Saiko-Fujiyoshida area offer lakefront rooms with postcard-perfect Fuji reflections. From here you can access the Kawaguchiko Ropeway, Oishi Park, and the Chureito Pagoda. Prices range from ¥8,000 for business hotels to ¥30,000+ for onsen ryokan.

Kofu Area - The capital of Yamanashi prefecture offers more affordable accommodation with easy access to Shosenkyo Gorge and Yamanashi wineries. Notable options include Hotel Mifuji, Hotel Crown Hills Kofu, and the stylish HOTEL R9 The Yard Kofu. Great for wine lovers, with several local wineries offering tastings.

Onsen & Ryokan Experience - For a traditional Japanese inn experience, look for properties with natural hot spring baths (onsen). Many ryokan in the Fuji Five Lakes area offer kaiseki dinner and tatami rooms. Booking a room with an onsen bath (kashikiri-buro) gives you a private soaking experience with mountain views.

Practical Tips - The best time to see Mt. Fuji clearly is early morning from October to February. Summer brings higher cloud coverage. The Fuji Subaru Line 5th Station (accessible June-September) is the starting point for climbing. Even if you don't climb, the drive up offers breathtaking alpine scenery. From Tokyo, take the Limited Express Azusa or Kaiji to Otsuki, then transfer to the Fujikyuko Line for Lake Kawaguchi (about 2 hours total).

Booking a hotel in Japan as a foreign tourist is easier than ever, but there are some important differences to be aware of. This guide covers everything from payment methods to check-in customs.

Payment Methods - Most hotels accept Visa and Mastercard. American Express and JCB are also widely accepted at mid-range and upscale properties. Budget business hotels and rural ryokan may be cash-only, so always carry yen. Booking online in advance lets you pay by card and confirm your room before arriving. Exchange rates shown on booking platforms are typically competitive.

Language Considerations - Don't worry if you don't speak Japanese. Major hotel chains (APA Hotel, Smile Hotel, Route Inn) have English booking sites and English-speaking staff. Our platform provides hotel descriptions in English, Japanese, Chinese, and Korean. When checking in, having your booking confirmation on your phone is helpful. Many hotels now use self check-in kiosks with multilingual support.

Cancellation Policies - Japanese hotels typically have stricter cancellation policies than Western ones. Many require cancellation 3-7 days before check-in for a full refund. "Non-refundable" plans (often labeled as "Early Bird" or "Special Rate") offer 10-30% savings but cannot be changed. Always read the cancellation terms before booking. Our platform shows cancellation policies clearly for each hotel.

Check-in & Check-out Times - Standard check-in is 3:00 PM and check-out is 10:00 AM in Japan, which is earlier than many countries. Some hotels offer luggage storage if you arrive early. Business hotels often have check-in from 2:00 PM. If you need late check-out, request it at check-in (many will extend to 11:00 AM free of charge).

Room Sizes - Japanese hotel rooms are typically smaller than Western standards. A "single" room usually has a semi-double bed (120-130cm wide). For two people, book a "double" or "twin" room. Family travelers should look specifically for family rooms or connectable rooms. Capsule hotels offer a uniquely Japanese experience for solo travelers on a budget.

Peak Seasons - Book at least 2-3 months ahead for cherry blossom season (late March to mid-April), autumn foliage (November), Golden Week (late April to early May), and New Year. Summer fireworks festivals and Obon (mid-August) are also busy periods. Off-peak travel (January-February, June) offers the best rates.
